# Finance, Fees, Billing, Cashiering and Subsidies: Philippine JHS–SHS (Private and Public Modes), current as of SY 2026–2027

Research date: 21 Nov 2026. deped.gov.ph and peac.org.ph/helpdesk pages returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches, so several figures come from secondary summaries of the primary orders. Each finding says where it came from, and the unverified ones are flagged.

## 1. Private schools: Manual of Regulations (DO 88 s.2010) on tuition and other fees, increases, refunds, collections, records/exam withholding, discounts

### Takeaway
DO 88 s.2010 (2010 Revised Manual of Regulations for Private Schools in Basic Education) still governs. I found no consolidated 2024–2026 replacement manual. Fee increases require consultation with parents and students and an application to the DepEd Regional Office. Fees stay at the previous year's rates until the increase is approved. RA 11984 ("No Permit, No Exam" prohibition) does cover K–12 for the whole school year, but only for learners certified as disadvantaged. Schools keep the right to require promissory notes and to withhold records/credentials, subject to DO 88's transfer-credential rules and a 2026 DepEd memo on graduation rites.

### Cited Findings
- DO 88 s.2010 is the 2010 Revised Manual of Regulations for Private Schools in Basic Education. It took effect from SY 2010–2011 — [DepEd issuance page](https://deped.gov.ph/?p=6543)
- In 2026, schools still file tuition/other-fee (TOSF) increase applications under DO 88 s.2010. Example: a Pasig private school says it is filing its AY 2026–2027 increase application under DO 88 and that fees stay at AY 2025–2026 levels until DepEd approves — [DLS-Zobel Enrollment Memo AY 2026–2027](https://www.dlszobel.edu.ph/?p=42051)
- Increase process: any proposed increase in tuition or other school fees, or any new fee, must go through consultation with the student government and parents. The application must carry an itemized statement of current TOSF rates and proof that consultation happened. Implementation is at the Regional Office level — [Manila Bulletin 2020](https://mb.com.ph/2020/05/09/deped-private-schools-need-to-justify-tuition-increase-before-approval); [respicio.ph commentary](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/can-a-private-school-increase-tuition-fees-in-the-middle-of-the-school-year). Some coverage cites "Section 181 of DO 88 s.2010 and its amendment through DO 11 s.[2011]" — [search snippet, depeddasma.edu.ph](https://www.depeddasma.edu.ph/?p=20525) (section number unverified)
- DepEd moves the consultation and application deadlines by memo from year to year. For SY 2023–2024 the consultation deadline moved from 30 Mar to 15 Jun 2023, and the application deadline from 1 Feb to 15 Apr 2023 — [Manila Bulletin Apr 2023](https://mb.com.ph/2023/4/9/dep-ed-adjusts-deadline-for-tuition-increase-application-renewal-of-permit-for-private-schools); [Philstar Apr 2023](https://philstar.com/nation/2023/04/09/2257611/deped-adjusts-tuition-fee-increase-consultation-deadline-private-schools). In Sept 2025 the consultation period was again reported extended — [Malaya, 15 Sep 2025](https://malaya.com.ph/news/consultation-of-private-schools-planning-to-raise-tuition-extended)
- Refunds on withdrawal: a secondary article places the rule in the Manual and gives this schedule: 10% of total fees charged if the learner withdraws in the first week of classes, 20% in the second week, full fees owed after that. A reservation fee (max 10% of tuition), if labelled non-refundable and DepEd-approved, is not refunded. The article blends DO 88 with the higher-education manual (MORPHE §102), so this is **unverified against DO 88 text** — [lawyer-philippines.com](https://www.lawyer-philippines.com/articles/tuition-fee-refund-before-school-year-starts-philippines)
- Withholding credentials: a DepEd FOI reply cites DO 88 s.2010 Sec. 128 "Withholding of Credentials" — [FOI request "LRN and Form 137"](https://foi.gov.ph/requests/lrn-and-form-137). A secondary commentary cites DO 88 Secs. 139–141. In its account, a certificate of eligibility to transfer certifies the learner is "relieved of financial and property responsibilities to the school", and once that certificate is issued the school must forward credentials — [respicio.ph](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/can-a-private-school-withhold-report-cards-for-unpaid-tuition). The section numbers conflict (128 vs 139–141) and are **unverified**.
- The same commentary cites **DepEd Memorandum No. 023, s. 2026**. Per that account, qualified learners must not be excluded from end-of-year rites "solely because of unsettled financial or property obligations". The memo also recognizes temporary enrollment at the receiving school when the private school withholds records, and requires credentials to be released immediately after the obligations are settled. Same source: DECS Order 63 s.1999 bars charging interest on unpaid tuition unless the enrollment contract expressly provides for it — [respicio.ph](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/can-a-private-school-withhold-report-cards-for-unpaid-tuition) (**secondary; not verified against the DepEd text**)
- Secondary sources conflict on whether private basic-ed schools may withhold Form 138/SF9 at all. Most say they may withhold for legitimate unpaid obligations, while at least one says basic-ed schools should not withhold Form 137/138 — [respicio.ph search results](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/can-schools-withhold-student-credentials-for-unpaid-tuition-in-the-philippines); [lawyer-philippines.com](https://www.lawyer-philippines.com/articles/understanding-deped-regulations-on-unpaid-tuition-and-school-records)
- RA 11984 (No Permit, No Exam Prohibition Act, signed March 2024) covers all public and private basic education (K–12), HEIs, and TVIs (long-term courses only). It bars policies that keep enrolled students from taking exams or any educational assessment because of unpaid financial obligations. "In the case of K to 12 students, the mandate shall be for the entire school year." — [RA 11984, LawPhil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11984_2024.html); [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1919598/new-law-scraps-no-permit-no-exam-rule)
- RA 11984 benefits disadvantaged students certified by the municipal/city/provincial social welfare officer or the DSWD regional office. It is "without prejudice to" the school's right to require a promissory note and to withhold records and credentials. Violations are subject to DepEd/CHED/TESDA administrative sanctions — [PIA, Mar 2024](https://pia.gov.ph/news/2024/03/26/pbbm-signs-law-allowing-disadvantaged-students-to-take-exams-despite-unpaid-school-fees); [PNA](https://alpha.pna.gov.ph/articles/1220951)
- A secondary source mentions 2026 IRR details for RA 11984: the Certificate of Disadvantaged Status is valid for one school year for K–12; processing takes 2 working days for calamity cases and 3 otherwise; denial-of-exam complaints must be filed within 7 calendar days — [respicio.ph](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/can-a-private-school-withhold-report-cards-for-unpaid-tuition) (**unverified; IRR not located on official site**)
- Mid-year increases without prior notice are generally not allowed. Approved fees apply to the school year as published — [respicio.ph](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/can-a-private-school-raise-tuition-fees-midyear-without-prior-notice) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Fee schedule versioning: the system should store a fee schedule per school year with a status (draft → consulted → filed → approved) and attach consultation evidence (minutes, attendance) and the approval reference. If the increase isn't approved by enrollment, the prior year's schedule should apply automatically.
- Exam blocking: never block exam or assessment participation on a balance. Credential release (SF10/Form 137, SF9/Form 138, certificate of eligibility to transfer) can be gated by clearance in private mode, but the gate needs an override and an audit trail. Record a "disadvantaged status certificate" flag with validity per SY, and a promissory note entity (amount, due date, signatory).
- Interest and penalties on late payments should be off by default and enabled only if the enrollment contract provides for them (DECS 63 s.1999, secondary).
- Refunds: build a configurable rule (week-of-withdrawal → % of total fees chargeable), defaulting to 10%/20%/100% but editable, because the DO 88 text wasn't verified.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve DO 88 s.2010's text (deped.gov.ph returns 403). Not verified: exact section numbers for fee increases, refunds, discounts, and credential withholding; the notice period for parents; whether the reservation fee is capped.
- No DepEd memo found that sets the SY 2026–2027 (or SY 2027–2028) consultation and application deadlines. They are issued yearly, so check the Regional Office or SDO.
- Discounts: no source found on DepEd rules for sibling, early-payment, or academic discounts in private basic ed. These appear to be school policy, but that is unconfirmed.
- Whether a 2024–2026 revision of the Manual exists: none found. Note that DO 12 s.2026 (government recognition for private SHS) and DO 11 s.2026 (E-GASTPE) amend adjacent areas — see §3.

## 2. Education Service Contracting (ESC), now under E-GASTPE (DO 11 s.2026)

### Takeaway
DO 11 s.2026 (signed 30 Apr 2026, effective SY 2026–2027) puts ESC, the SHS Voucher Program and the Teachers' Salary Subsidy (TSS) into one revised E-GASTPE framework. It repeals DO 20 s.2017 and DO 20 s.2023. ESC amounts are unchanged: ₱13,000 (NCR), ₱11,000 (HUCs outside NCR), ₱9,000 (elsewhere) per grantee per year. The school is paid the lower of the grant and its actual fees. Entry is at Grade 7 only, with four priority categories. TSS rose from ₱18,000 to ₱24,000.

### Cited Findings
- DO 11 s.2026, "Revised Guidelines on the Implementation of E-GASTPE", dated 30 Apr 2026 — [DepEd issuance page](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2026/04/30/april-30-2026-do-011-s-2026-revised-guidelines-on-the-implementation-of-expanded-government-assistance-to-students-and-teachers-in-private-education-e-gastpe-in-the-department-of-educat/) (title only; page 403 on fetch)
- It consolidates ESC, SHS VP and TSS. It prioritizes low-income, IP and geographically isolated learners and adds stricter screening and monitoring. Targets for SY 2026–2027: ~990,000 ESC and ~1.47M SHS VP beneficiaries (>2.4M total). TSS goes from ₱18,000 to ₱24,000 — [GMA News, 1 May 2026](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/986030/deped-targets-2-4m-private-school-subsidy-beneficiaries-for-sy-2026-2027/story/)
- ESC amounts per DO 11 s.2026 Sec. 21, Table 5: NCR ₱13,000; HUC outside NCR ₱11,000; all other cities/municipalities ₱9,000. The amount fixed at Grade 7 applies for all four JHS years. Sec. 83 repeals DO 20 s.2017 and DO 20 s.2023 — [schoolfinderph.com ESC guide](https://www.schoolfinderph.com/blog/esc-grant-private-school) (secondary, cites the signed order; **not checked against the PDF**)
- If the school's declared total fees in the ESC IMS are below the grant, the school is paid only its fees. The grant "shall not be construed as a scholarship or a tuition fee discount". TSS is ₱24,000 per eligible teacher (Sec. 26), funded from the ESC component — [schoolfinderph.com](https://www.schoolfinderph.com/blog/esc-grant-private-school) (secondary)
- Eligibility (Secs. 7–8, Table 1): Grade 6 completers entering Grade 7 at a participating private JHS, subject to slots. Priority categories: A Social Equity Groups (poor/near-poor per CBMS, 4Ps, IPs, PWDs, etc.); B public-school completers from poor to middle-middle income households (congested-school learners first); C ALS A&E/PEPT passers; D private-school completers. Within each category, the order is poor > low-income > lower-middle > middle-middle. Sec. 52 allows upper-grade entrants only in exceptional cases — [schoolfinderph.com](https://www.schoolfinderph.com/blog/esc-grant-private-school) (secondary)
- For SY 2026–2027, learners in upper grade levels cannot be accepted into ESC — [PEAC search snippet](https://peac.org.ph/?p=18392)
- Retention conditions: the grantee stays in the same participating school, is promoted each year, and keeps passing grades. The grant can end on drop-out (except documented health reasons), on failing and repeating a grade, or on suspension longer than two weeks. An ESC grantee is no longer automatically a voucher recipient. They apply as SHS VP Category D — [schoolfinderph.com](https://www.schoolfinderph.com/blog/esc-grant-private-school) (secondary)
- Learners apply through the participating school, and ESC application windows are school-run. Example: one SY 2025–2026 Grade 7 ESC application closed 18 July — [LSGH](https://www.lsgh.edu.ph/esc-application-for-school-year-2025-2026-now-open/); [PEAC Helpdesk: Learner Eligibility in ESC](https://helpdesk.peac.org.ph/hc/en-us/articles/16860040311951-Learner-Eligibility-and-Application-in-the-ESC-Program)
- Billing timeline for SY 2026–2027 (DepEd Memorandum No. 031 s.2026, dated 18 May 2026, attached to SDO Muntinlupa Memo 223 s.2026). ESC and SHS VP: VMS/IMS open for billing-statement creation 1 Aug 2026 → creation deadline 7 Oct 2026 → PEAC Regional Secretariat 14 Oct → DepEd RO 19 Oct → PEAC National Secretariat 26 Oct → DepEd GASS-ODMD 30 Oct 2026. TSS: IMS opens 1 Feb 2027 → creation 31 Mar → PEAC RS 12 Apr → DepEd RO 16 Apr → PEAC NS 21 Apr → GASS-ODMD 30 Apr 2027 — [SDO Muntinlupa NUM-2026-223 (PDF)](https://deped-muntinlupa.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NUM-2026-223.pdf)
- PEAC role: PEAC National Secretariat and Regional Secretariats process billing statements in the ESC IMS and SHS VP VMS (same memo). PEAC also runs ESC-linked school-improvement programs (e.g., CoRE coaching for ESC-participating JHS) — [PEAC CoRE](https://peac.org.ph/core/); [PEAC Helpdesk billing schedule SY 2026–2027](https://helpdesk.peac.org.ph/hc/en-us/articles/16899225495695-Schedule-for-Creating-GASTPE-Billing-Statements-in-SY-2026-2027) (title only; 403)
- Scale reference: PEAC processed ~3,632–3,657 participating JHS and ~900,000 grantees in SY 2023–2024 — [schoolfinderph.com](https://www.schoolfinderph.com/blog/esc-grant-private-school) (secondary)
- New law: Congress ratified a "Basic Education Voucher Assistance Act" in Sept 2026, extending vouchers to Kindergarten–Grade 6 and to middle-income households. It adds estafa penalties, fines up to ₱500,000 and restitution for fraudulent claims, and creates a Bureau of Private Education in DepEd. DepEd's SY 2027–2028 proposal is ₱4.34B for >292,000 learners in 2,632 schools. As of the article, no RA number or signing date had been reported — [Daily Tribune, 23 Sep 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/23/expanded-voucher-law-extends-private-school-aid-to-elementary-learners)

### Inferences
- Subsidy engine: model "grant programs" (ESC, SHS VP, TSS) with per-SY rate tables keyed by school location tier (NCR / HUC / other). Amount credited per learner = min(rate, total assessed fees). Post the credit to the learner ledger as a receivable from the government (PEAC/DepEd), not as a discount. The family pays the remainder ("top-up").
- Grantee lifecycle: application (category A–D plus income band and evidence) → committee ranking → slot allocation → grantee status per SY → continuation checks (promoted, same school, not suspended >2 weeks) → termination reasons.
- Billing export: per-term or per-SY billing-statement worksheet matching IMS/VMS fields, with a deadline calendar seeded from the yearly DepEd memo (e.g., 1 Aug–7 Oct). Track the government receivable through to payment.
- TSS is a teacher-payroll pass-through: track eligible teachers and the ₱24,000 annual amount (said to be paid "for ten months"; exact disbursement mechanics unverified).
- The new voucher law may widen subsidies to elementary from SY 2027–2028, which is out of scope for a JHS–SHS school but argues for keeping grant types configurable.

### Gaps
- DO 11 s.2026's PDF was not retrievable, so the amounts, section numbers, and the ESC fee threshold/eligibility of schools come from one secondary site plus news.
- No per-region (Region I–XIII) ESC table found. The only tiers are NCR / HUC / other.
- No SY 2026–2027 slot counts per school. No detailed school-side reporting list (e.g., enrollment confirmation, grantee attendance) beyond the billing workflow.
- Whether ESC billing is one annual statement or split by semester/term for SY 2026–2027: the memo shows one billing window (Aug–Oct).

## 3. Senior High School Voucher Program (SHS VP)

### Takeaway
Voucher values for SY 2026–2027 (DO 11 s.2026, Sec. 48, Table 13, via a secondary source) keep the old peso amounts but re-map categories. Categories A, B and C get NCR ₱22,500, HUC ₱20,000, others ₱17,500. Categories D and E get NCR ₱18,000, HUC ₱16,000, others ₱14,000. The school receives the lower of its total fees and the voucher. Nobody is "automatically qualified" any more; categories A–D apply at the school and E applies online via OVAP. No new Grade 12 vouchers are issued for SY 2026–2027. From SY 2027–2028, only private SHS with full Government Recognition (DO 12 s.2026) may participate.

### Cited Findings
- Voucher maxima per beneficiary per SY (DO 11 s.2026 Sec. 48 Table 13): NCR: A/B/C ₱22,500, D/E ₱18,000. HUC outside NCR: A/B/C ₱20,000, D/E ₱16,000. Other locations: A/B/C ₱17,500, D/E ₱14,000. Peso amounts match the repealed DO 20 s.2023 table — [schoolfinderph.com SHS VP guide](https://www.schoolfinderph.com/blog/shs-voucher-program-guide) (secondary; **unverified against PDF**). A pre-2026 Mapúa page lists ESC grantees as "Category B" at NCR ₱18,000 / HUC ₱16,000, which reflects the old lettering — [Mapúa support KB](https://support.mapua.edu.ph/kb/article/152/deped-senior-high-school-voucher-program). **Category letters differ between old and new rules; use the DO 11 s.2026 mapping.**
- Categories (Sec. 33): A Grade 10 completers in Social Equity Groups (CBMS poor/near-poor, 4Ps, GIDCA, IPs, PWD/LSEN), other than ALS. B public JHS completers from poor to middle-middle income. C ALS A&E/PEPT Grade 10 passers, poor to middle-middle. D Grade 10 ESC grantees. These four file at the school (Table 8), in priority order A first. E private-school Grade 10 completers (non-ESC), poor to middle-middle, apply online via OVAP (Table 9), and are processed only after A–D, subject to funds and capacity. There is no Category F. "Qualified Voucher Recipient" now means found eligible after applying (Sec. 4) — [schoolfinderph.com](https://www.schoolfinderph.com/blog/shs-voucher-program-guide) (secondary)
- ALS A&E passers from poor to middle-income households no longer apply through OVAP. They submit documents to the chosen VP-participating SHS during enrollment, and the SHS VP school committee screens and approves them subject to priority, capacity and program limits. Voucher redemption runs 3 Aug 2026 – 3 Nov 2026, and unredeemed vouchers are forfeited — [DepEd Memo 031 s.2026 via SDO Muntinlupa NUM-2026-223](https://deped-muntinlupa.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NUM-2026-223.pdf)
- School process: the SHS VP School Committee evaluates within 10 working days of the application deadline and notifies ineligible applicants in writing within 3 working days. For Category E, minor deficiencies can be corrected within 10 working days of results, and redemption begins 30 working days after the QVA Certificate is issued (Secs. 33–41). Subsidy covers two consecutive years starting Grade 11 (Sec. 31). PhilSys ID verification phases in and becomes mandatory from SY 2029–2030 (Sec. 51) — [schoolfinderph.com](https://www.schoolfinderph.com/blog/shs-voucher-program-guide) (secondary)
- Payment rule: the school is paid "based on the total school fees it charges or the applicable maximum voucher, whichever is lower". Any excess fees are paid by the family to the school — [schoolfinderph.com](https://www.schoolfinderph.com/blog/shs-voucher-program-guide) (secondary)
- For SY 2026–2027, a Grade 12 learner cannot be accepted into the SHS VP, because no DepEd order authorized new Grade 12 vouchers. Billing creation opens 1 Aug 2026 and the deadline is 7 Oct 2026 — [WebSearch summary of PEAC helpdesk / GMA](https://helpdesk.peac.org.ph/hc/en-us/articles/16870665777679-Learner-Eligibility-and-Application-in-the-SHS-Voucher-Program); [SDO Muntinlupa memo](https://deped-muntinlupa.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/NUM-2026-223.pdf)
- DO 12 s.2026, "Government Recognition of Private Basic Education Institutions Offering the SHS Program": private SHS must shift to full Government Recognition by SY 2027–2028. Under DO 11 Secs. 45–46, only recognized SHS may take part in the VP from SY 2027–2028 — [SDO Muntinlupa NUM-2026-269](https://deped-muntinlupa.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NUM-2026-269.pdf); [Daily Tribune, 7 May 2026](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/05/07/deped-orders-full-shift-to-government-recognition-for-private-senior-high-schools-by-20272028); [schoolfinderph.com](https://www.schoolfinderph.com/blog/shs-voucher-program-guide)
- The Strengthened SHS Curriculum is fully implemented for incoming Grade 11 from SY 2026–2027, and Grade 12 continues on the existing curriculum (DepEd memo, 27 Feb 2026, enclosed in SDO Muntinlupa memo) — [NUM-2026-269 enclosure](https://deped-muntinlupa.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NUM-2026-269.pdf)
- OVAP application page (Category E) — [PEAC Voucher Application](https://peac.org.ph/voucher-application/)

### Inferences
- Voucher table keyed by (SY, location tier, category group {A–C, D–E}). Credit = min(voucher, total fees). Excess goes to the family as "top-up". Neither primary nor secondary sources I found set a cap on top-up fees under DO 11; the only constraint found is the min() rule plus normal DepEd TOSF approval.
- Fields to store per voucher learner: category, evidence docs, QVA certificate number (for E), redemption date (must fall inside the redemption window, e.g., 3 Aug–3 Nov 2026), LRN/PhilSys number, SHS strand/track, and a Grade 11 → Grade 12 continuation flag.
- Guard: block voucher assignment for a learner first enrolled at Grade 12 in SY 2026–2027. Warn if the school's Government Recognition status isn't set for SY 2027–2028.

### Gaps
- Primary DO 11 s.2026 Table 13 not seen. The amounts rely on one secondary site whose numbers match the earlier DO 20 s.2023 values.
- No explicit top-up fee ceiling found for 2026 (older rules had limits for some school types; not confirmed).
- No figures found for public SHS / SUC / LUC voucher amounts (older rules gave lower rates for non-DepEd public SHS). Not relevant to private mode, but unverified.
- PEAC VMS reporting requirements beyond billing (e.g., attendance, drop-out reporting) not found.

## 4. Public schools: no-collection policy, contributions, MOOE, PTA, canteen

### Takeaway
Public JHS/SHS charge no tuition and may not make any collection a condition of enrollment, promotion or clearance. Contributions are voluntary, governed by DO 41 s.2012 (and earlier DO 20 s.2006 / DO 44 s.2002). PTA funds belong to the PTA, not the school, under DO 13 s.2022 (Omnibus PTA Guidelines). Teachers and school officials must not collect them. The school's own money is the MOOE (DO 13 s.2016 direct release; DO 12 s.2014 utilization), handled through government accounting. A public-mode module therefore needs no cashiering of student fees. At most it can track MOOE budget and liquidation and log voluntary/PTA/canteen items for transparency.

### Cited Findings
- The "no collection policy" is stated in DO 41 s.2012, which includes implementing policies on voluntary school contributions — [Inquirer](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/7457/deped-reminds-public-schools-on-%e2%80%98no-collection%e2%80%99-policy/amp); [PNA](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1036691)
- Non-payment of voluntary contributions "shall not be used as a basis for non-admission, non-promotion, or non-issuance of clearance". PTA collections are voluntary, agreed in a general assembly, and teachers and school officials should not be involved in collecting them. The PTA submits a financial report on how the previous year's collections were used — [PNA](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1071316); [Manila Bulletin 2018](https://mb.com.ph/2018/05/21/deped-reminds-principals-teachers-on-no-collection-policy)
- No collection of fees during enrollment or school opening — [Philstar 2014](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2014/05/29/1328564/deped-school-execs-no-collection-fees-during-enrollment). DepEd also repeats the rule for Christmas parties and graduation (graduation fees banned) — [GMA](https://gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/392586/deped-reminds-schools-of-no-collection-policy-for-christmas-party/story); [Philstar 2006](https://www.philstar.com/metro/2006/03/03/324050/deped-bans-collection-graduation-fees). In Nov 2023 DepEd said it would issue a new DO reiterating the policy — [ABS-CBN](https://www.abs-cbn.com/video/news/11/10/23/deped-reminds-schools-of-no-collection-rule)
- Older bases: DO 20 s.2006 (voluntary collection of fees in public elementary/secondary schools) — [DepEd](https://www.deped.gov.ph/?p=9327); DO 44 s.2002 (collection of fees by PTCAs) — [DepEd](https://deped.gov.ph/?p=4779)
- DO 13 s.2022, Omnibus Guidelines on the Regulation of Operations of PTAs (March 2022): covers all public schools offering K–12, with allowed and prohibited PTA activities, dispute resolution and sanctions — [Manila Bulletin, 11 Apr 2022](https://mb.com.ph/2022/04/11/updated-guidelines-on-parents-teachers-associations-issued-by-deped); [DepEd](https://deped.gov.ph/?p=113681)
- School MOOE: DO 13 s.2016 (direct release and use of school MOOE allocations); DO 12 s.2014 (guidelines and procedures for school MOOE utilization) — [DepEd](https://deped.gov.ph/?p=5212); [search result](https://www.slideshare.net/Ryan135454/edma-504cunanan-ryan-augusto10292022pptx)

### Inferences
- In public mode, turn off tuition, assessments, the cashier, and holds/clearance on balances. Optionally keep: (a) an MOOE allocation/disbursement/liquidation log for the school head and bookkeeper; (b) a PTA/voluntary-contribution transparency register maintained by PTA officers, not teachers, which never links to learner status; (c) canteen income records if the school runs one.
- Hard rule in code: in public mode, no learner-facing balance may block enrollment, promotion, SF9/SF10 release or clearance.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm the current canteen issuance (commonly cited as DO 8 s.2007 and DO 13 s.2017 on healthy food in canteens) or rules on canteen income use. Not verified.
- Could not confirm whether DepEd issued the promised post-2023 order reiterating no-collection.
- Text of DO 13 s.2022 on PTA fund custody (bank accounts, signatories, reporting periods) not retrieved.
- Newer MOOE guidance (a DO 8 s.2019 revision is often cited) not verified.

## 5. Receipts and tax: BIR invoicing, VAT, income tax, EIS/e-invoicing deadline

### Takeaway
Educational services of DepEd-accredited/recognized private schools are VAT-exempt (NIRC Sec. 109(1)(H)), but non-educational sales such as canteen rentals and asset disposals may be VATable. Proprietary schools pay 10% preferential income tax (1% from 1 Jul 2020 to 30 Jun 2023). Non-stock non-profit (NSNP) schools are exempt on income used actually, directly and exclusively for education. The BIR e-invoicing deadline is 31 Dec 2026 (RR 26-2025 amending RR 11-2025; RMC 98-2026 keeps it). It covers specific groups (large taxpayers, e-commerce, exporters, CAS users, per secondary sources) and excludes micro taxpayers. Whether a given private school is covered depends on its classification, not on being a school.

### Cited Findings
- VAT exemption: "Educational services rendered by private educational institutions duly accredited by DepEd, CHED or TESDA, and government educational institutions are exempt from the VAT". ### Inferences
- Under the Ease of Paying Taxes Act (RA 11976, from memory, not sourced here), the "invoice" replaced the "official receipt" for services from 2024. ## 6. Common payment practices: installments, channels, promissory notes

### Takeaway
I found no DepEd rule mandating particular installment schedules or payment channels. Installment plans (annual, semestral, quarterly, monthly), online channels and promissory notes are school practice. RA 11984 explicitly preserves the use of promissory notes, and DECS 63 s.1999 (secondary) limits interest on arrears to what the contract provides.

### Cited Findings
- RA 11984 preserves schools' power to require a promissory note — [PIA](https://pia.gov.ph/news/2024/03/26/pbbm-signs-law-allowing-disadvantaged-students-to-take-exams-despite-unpaid-school-fees); [LawPhil](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2024/ra_11984_2024.html)
- A promissory note may support exam access or a settlement but doesn't by itself compel release of records. Interest on unpaid tuition requires an express contract term (DECS Order 63 s.1999) — [respicio.ph](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/can-a-private-school-withhold-report-cards-for-unpaid-tuition) (secondary)
- Example of school-published enrollment and payment terms for AY 2026–2027 — [DLS-Zobel Enrollment Memo](https://www.dlszobel.edu.ph/?p=42051)

### Inferences
- Support payment plans (cash/annual, semestral, quarterly, monthly), each generating a due-date schedule. Plan choice may carry an installment surcharge or a cash discount, both governed by the approved fee schedule.
- Payment channels: cashier (cash/check), bank deposit or transfer, and e-wallets (GCash/Maya) through a gateway or reference-number reconciliation. Plan for import and reconciliation of bank and e-wallet statements.
- Subsidy credits (ESC/VP) should be applied to the schedule before computing the family's installments, or proportionally. Government payment arrives months after billing (Aug–Oct submission), so treat it as a separate receivable.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source found on typical quarterly schedules or on GCash/Maya use by schools. Treat these as product assumptions to confirm with pilot schools.
